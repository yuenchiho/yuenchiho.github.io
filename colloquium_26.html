  • 22nd September

    Speaker: Prof. Zih-Yin Chen (National Taiwan University)
    Title: A Particle-scale understanding of the collapse of a granular raft
    Abstract: Fluid-fluid interfaces laden with discrete particles behave like continuous elastic sheets, leading to their applications in emulsion and foam stabilization. Although current continuum models can qualitatively describe the elastic buckling of granular particle-laden interfaces—often referred to as granular rafts—under compression, the connection between their macroscopic collective properties and the microscopic behavior of individual particles remains unclear. Here, by combining systematic experiments with first-principle modeling, we aim to reveal how the macroscopic mechanical properties of particle rafts emerge from particle-scale interactions and predict the failure modes of a granular raft under compression. Our study highlights the dual nature of particle rafts and exemplifies how collective dynamics can arise from discrete components with simple interactions.

    Speaker: Prof. Farbod Shokrieh (University of Washington)
    Title: Graphs in algebraic and arithmetic geometry
    Abstract: Graphs can be viewed as (non-archimedean) analogs of Riemann surfaces. For example, there is a notion of Jacobians for graphs. More classically, graphs can be viewed as electrical networks. I will explain the interplay between these points of view and some recent applications in algebraic and arithmetic geometry.

    Speaker: Prof. Jing Huang (University of Victoria)
    Title: On graphs which are locally complete 2-edge-colourable
    Abstract: A 2-edge-colouring of a graph G is called locally complete if for each vertex v, the vertices adjacent to v through edges of the same colour induce a complete subgraph in G. Contreras-Balbuena et al characterized locally complete 2-edge-coloured graphs which have alternating Hamiltonian cycles. Chvátal and Sbihi proved that graphs which are locally complete 2-edge-colourable are one of two types of claw-free perfect graphs indecomposable via clique-cutsets. Maffray and Reed gave a forbidden subgraph characterization of these graphs.
        We compare locally complete 2-edge-colourable graphs with proper interval graphs and proper circular-arc graphs. We characterize proper interval graphs and proper circular-arc graphs which are locally complete 2-edge-colourable by forbidden subgraphs.
        This is a joint work with Jørgen Bang-Jensen.

    Speaker: Prof. Donggyu Kim (Hanyang University)
    Title: Embedded graphs, Eulerian digraphs, and their Jacobian groups
    Abstract: The celebrated Kirchhoff matrix-tree theorem connects a combinatorial quantity---the number of spanning trees of a graph---with an algebraic one---the determinant of its reduced Laplacian matrix. This theorem has analogues for Eulerian digraphs and embedded graphs, where determinants of certain matrices count Eulerian tours and quasi-trees, respectively. We will explore the interplay between these two settings and see how the corresponding matrices lead to Jacobian groups that naturally act on Eulerian tours and quasi-trees.

    Speaker: Prof. Wai-Kit Lam (National Taiwan University)
    Title: Noise sensitivity of Boolean functions
    Abstract: A Boolean function is a function from the discrete hypercube to {-1, 1} or {0, 1}. Such a function can be viewed as a voting system: each person has either opinion -1 or 1, and the function tells us the result after knowing all the opinions. In this talk, we will focus on whether a Boolean function is noise sensitive, in the sense that the result can be easily interrupted by a small amount of noise. We will then talk about Boolean functions that arise from percolation, a model in statistical physics, and an interesting implication of noise sensitivity of this model.

  • 3rd November

    Speaker: Prof. Yen-Chi Lee (National Central University)
    Title: From First-Hitting Locations to Gaussian Variance Mixtures: Channel Structure and Capacity Scaling
    Abstract: The location at which a diffusing particle first reaches a boundary provides a natural link between stochastic geometry and non-Gaussian probability laws. In this talk, we consider Brownian motion with constant drift normal to an absorbing hyperplane and study its transverse first-hitting location. The resulting distribution admits a Gaussian variance-mixture representation, revealing how a random hitting time generates a family of spatial laws that approaches a Cauchy distribution as the drift vanishes. We then explore the information-theoretic consequences by treating this boundary-induced randomness as additive channel noise. Under a second-moment input constraint, we obtain an exact capacity expansion in the large-input-power limit: the logarithmic growth coefficient is determined by the boundary dimension, while the constant term depends on the transport parameters only through the noise entropy. The talk connects first-hitting distributions, Gaussian mixtures, and capacity scaling through a common geometric and probabilistic framework.
